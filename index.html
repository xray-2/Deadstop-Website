The problem

You didn't come to the gym to scroll.

Every rest timer tells you to rest 90 seconds. Almost nobody actually does. You finish a set, pick up your phone to check the clock, and a notification is waiting. Ninety seconds turns into five minutes. Five minutes turns into your whole session.

It's not a willpower problem. Your phone is built to keep you scrolling, and no amount of discipline beats that after a long day. The only fix that actually works is taking the phone away, and giving it back the moment your rest is over.

How it works

Three steps, then it runs itself.

01

Pick your distractions

Choose the apps that get you every time: Instagram, TikTok, X, whatever it is for you. iOS keeps your choice private. Deadstop never sees which apps you picked.

02

Rest like normal

Log your set and the clock starts. That's your only phone time until it runs out. Pick how strict it should be, from none at all to a short grace period.

03

The block kicks in

When your rest ends, those apps lock. Not a reminder you can swipe away. A real block, built into iOS, that holds even if you force-quit Deadstop.

The app

Everything else, kept simple.

01 At a glance

Deadstop is an iPhone app that runs a rest timer between your working sets and blocks distracting apps while that timer runs. It is a personal tool you use on yourself. It is not a parental control, not a monitoring product, and it reports to nobody.

The short version of everything below:

  • No account. You never create one, and we have nothing to attach data to.
  • No analytics or tracking. The app contains no analytics SDK, no advertising SDK, and no tracking technology. We do not track you across apps or websites, and we do not ask for permission to, because we have no use for it.
  • No sale or sharing. We do not sell personal information, share it for cross-context behavioural advertising, or provide it to data brokers.
  • One exception, and it is Apple's. Buying Deadstop Pro creates a purchase record handled by Apple and by RevenueCat, our subscription provider. That record is described in section 07.

02 What stays on your device

Deadstop stores the following on your iPhone, in the app's own storage and in an Apple App Group shared with its extensions. None of it is uploaded, backed up to us, or readable by us:

  • Your training split, exercises, day names and per-day rest settings.
  • Your session history: sets logged, rest prescribed, rest actually taken, and session start and end times.
  • Your settings: strictness, budget mode, alert preferences.
  • The opaque Screen Time token representing your app selection (section 03).
  • Your gym's coordinates and radius, if you set one (section 05).
  • Health values read from Apple Health for display (section 04).
  • Timing measurements used by the in-app Diagnostics screen (section 08).

This data may be included in your personal iCloud or encrypted local backup of the device, in the same way every other app's data is. That backup belongs to you and is governed by Apple's privacy policy, not ours. We have no access to it.

03 Screen Time & app blocking

The blocking in Deadstop is performed by Apple's Screen Time system, specifically the Family Controls, Managed Settings and Device Activity frameworks, using the individual authorisation mode, in which you restrict your own device.

Deadstop cannot see which apps you selected. When you pick apps, iOS presents its own picker and returns an opaque token, not a list. That token is meaningless outside your device and outside Deadstop, cannot be decoded into app names by us or by anyone else, and is stored locally so the blocking rules can be applied.

Stated plainly

Screen Time data never leaves your device. Deadstop does not transmit, log, sell or analyse your app selections or your device usage. It is never used for advertising, profiling, market research or any secondary purpose. It is used for one thing: applying and lifting the restriction you asked for, while you train.

Deadstop also uses a usage threshold from the Device Activity framework when Scroll Seconds is enabled, so the app can tell how much of your rest budget has been spent inside the blocked apps. That measurement is evaluated on the device by an Apple extension, is reported to Deadstop only as elapsed time against your budget, and is not retained afterward.

Screen Time access is optional. If you decline it, Deadstop still runs as a rest timer. It simply cannot block anything. You can revoke it at any time from Settings inside the app, which immediately releases every restriction, or from iOS Settings.

04 Apple Health data

If you grant Health access, Deadstop uses HealthKit in two directions, both entirely on your device.

What it writes

Each finished session is saved to Apple Health as a strength training workout, with its start time, end time and duration, so it appears in Fitness and counts toward your activity rings like any other workout.

What it reads

Deadstop reads resting heart rate, heart rate variability, sleep and body mass, and displays them alongside your rest data so recovery and training can be read together. These values are fetched from HealthKit for display and are never uploaded.

Health data commitments

Health data obtained through HealthKit is never used for advertising or marketing, never used for any form of data mining or resale, and never disclosed to any third party, including advertisers, analytics providers, data brokers or insurers. It is not shared with our payment provider. It is used solely to present your own information back to you inside the app.

Health access is optional and granular. You control each category in the Health app under Sharing, and the rest timer and blocking work whether you grant it or not. Revoking access stops Deadstop reading further data immediately. Workouts Deadstop previously wrote remain in Apple Health until you delete them there, because they are your records, held in Apple's app rather than ours.

05 Location

Deadstop can start a session on its own when you arrive at your gym. To do that, you mark your gym once, and iOS watches for you entering that region.

Your gym's coordinates and radius are stored on your device. The geofence is registered with iOS and monitored by the operating system, which wakes Deadstop only to say that a boundary was crossed. Your location is never transmitted to us, never logged as a history, and never used to build a movement profile. Deadstop does not track where you are. It knows one place: the one you told it.

When you search for your gym on the map, that search is handled by Apple Maps and is subject to Apple's privacy policy. Location access is optional; declining it only means sessions start when you tap to start them. You can change or revoke it in iOS Settings at any time, and deleting your saved gym removes the geofence.

06 Notifications and alarms

Deadstop's alerts for rest ending and for arriving at your gym are local notifications and alarms scheduled on your device. There is no push server, no push token, and nothing is sent over the network to deliver them. They are posted as time-sensitive so they can reach you through Focus and silent mode, which matters when the phone is in a pocket and a set is waiting.

08 Diagnostics you choose to send

Deadstop measures how quickly a block is reapplied after a rest ends, and shows you those timings in Settings under Diagnostics. Those measurements live on your device and are not collected by us.

That screen has an export control, which produces a plain CSV file of the raw timings and hands it to the iOS share sheet. Nothing is sent unless you use it, and where it goes is entirely your choice. If you send it to us to help diagnose a problem, we use it only for that and delete it once the issue is resolved. It contains timing data, not app names, health data or location.
